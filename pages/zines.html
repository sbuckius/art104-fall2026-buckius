The Ten — our reflection tools are named after these

The Comet1930
Science Correspondence Club, Chicago
Widely cited as the first science-fiction fanzine. The moment fans stopped only reading the future and started printing their own.
Week 1 · Solarpunk
fanac.org/fanzines/Comet
The Witch and the Chameleon1974
Amanda Bankier, Canada
One of the first science-fiction fanzines centered on women. Made because there was no space for women in the fandom, so she built one.
Week 2 · Permacomputing
wikipedia.org · The Witch and the Chameleon
Slant1948
Walt Willis & James White, Belfast
Hand-set on a letterpress in a spare room, one line of type at a time. Craft as devotion when there was no easy way to print.
Week 3 · Ecomedia
fanac.org/fanzines/Slant
Aurora1975
SF³ / Janus, Madison
A feminist science-fiction zine (formerly Janus) from the Wisconsin women's SF community that also founded WisCon.
Week 5 · Energy Humanities
sf3.org · Janus / Aurora covers
Slingshot1988–
Long Haul Infoshop, Berkeley
A radical quarterly printed on newsprint, still running, still local. The organizer behind the famous DIY pocket planner.
Week 6 · Discard Studies
slingshotcollective.org
Evolution of a Race Riot1997
Mimi Thi Nguyen
A landmark compilation zine of writing by people of color in punk, made in response to the whiteness of the Riot Grrrl scene.
Week 7 · Maintenance & Care
issuu.com · POC Zine Project
Shotgun Seamstress2006
Osa Atoe, Portland & New Orleans
A cut-and-paste fanzine by and for Black punks, born from being the only Black kid at the show. Eight issues, now collected by Soft Skull.
Week 9 · Afrofuturism
softskull.com · Shotgun Seamstress
Energumen1970

A Hugo-winning fanzine of the offset-printing era, known for its art and its letter column — the social network before the internet.

fanac.org/fanzines/Energumen
Khatru1975

Famous for its 1975 Women in Science Fiction symposium — a printed debate among Le Guin, Russ, Tiptree and others about gender and the genre.

Hyphen1952
Chuck Harris & Walt Willis, Belfast
The great mid-century letter-and-conversation zine, built almost entirely around its readers' voices writing back.
Week 13 · Testimony
fanac.org/fanzines/Hyphen

A note on the lineage

The oldest zine here is from 1930 and the newest from 2006, and they were made by science-fiction fans, feminists, Black punks, and Belfast letterpress obsessives. What they share is not a subject — it's a method: make it yourself, make it cheap, hand it to someone.

That method is what you'll practice all term, and what your final project becomes.
