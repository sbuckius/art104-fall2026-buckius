We center every person in the chain: those who design the machines, build them, train them, use them, and are left to live with what they leave behind. Technology does not exist outside of human hands and human consequences — and a caring technological future accounts for every person in that chain, not just the ones at the top of it.\n2\nCARE-centered Humans CARE. Machines do not CARE. Humans must design technology from a perspective of human empathy, consideration, and community by including the voices of diverse people and centering human wellbeing across the full spectrum of human experience.\n3\nChallenging bias Technology is not neutral. We must name and dismantle the structural inequities baked into technological systems, datasets, and design choices.\n4\nUnderamplified histories Not all stories are amplified and shared. We uncover and share the contributions of those whose work has been erased, credited to others, or never recorded at all.\n5\nEquitable futures Agency, Power, and Access. We must build a technology landscape where diverse voices don't just contribute, but shape the direction — one carebot at a time.\n6\nCaring for the world Technology impacts the enivornment and world. We must ask what technologies we owe the planet and which technologies harm it.\n7\nHuman wisdom Humans have many types of complex knowledge. Machines can compute, but do not possess values, ethics, morals, feelings, common sense, context, understanding, opinions, awareness, passion, experience, empathy, CARE. When working with machines, humans should retain the authority, decision-making power, vision, and imagination. Machines do not posses the wisdom of humans. Human knowledge lives in hands, in judgment, in care. We must work to understand what must never be handed to machines and to ensure what is automated augments people so that they can do more of what only humans can: listening, grieving, connecting, deciding together.\n8\nPlayful, absurd resistanceAbsurdity can reveal the irony, challenges, concerns, and power structures in systems. We hope to challenge the dominant paradigms of technoptimism, technological determinism, technocapitalism, and inevitable march of technological progress.
