☘ AI, Your Voice, and Honesty ▼

My goal for you in this class is deep learning, creative thinking, and the development of your creative voice. In my opinion, AI is not recommended in some situations in the academic environment because it does not always support a process that facilitates the fullest, deepest learning potential that you can reach.

AI can have some important applications in the world if it 1) has good training data, 2) there is work to reduce bias, 3) it is deployed in an equitable manner, 4) the power over its design and implementation is not held in the hands of a select few individuals and corporations, 5) it supports deep and wide learning, and 6) it is designed and implemented in ways that support humans (and the earth) now and in the future. If you choose to use it in this class, be cautious and critical of the results and the larger societal impact — AI results can be inaccurate, biased, narrow, exclusionary, and filled with mistakes, and the system can be exploitative of human labor and potentially harmful to the environment.

In this class, I encourage you to use your own mind and imagination when it comes to your personal opinions, lived experiences, and the rendering of the final images for speculative artworks, because this is where art can really have an impact and emerge from you in novel ways. This is your creative voice. If you choose to use AI, think critically about whether it is replacing your creative voice or supporting it. Be aware of when it might do neither — or both.

From my perspective, the bottom line in this class is critical thinking, awareness, and learning when it comes to AI use. Always ask yourself: Is it supporting my learning and critical thinking? Is it helping me to construct my creative voice that is specific to me and novel due to my lived experience, thoughts, opinions, and ideas?

Some AI tools can be thought of as automation. When you use certain AI tools, consider replacing the term "AI" with "automation." Do you think using these automation tools in this context serves your learning and creative voice?

OVERVIEW

→ I WILL NOT be using any AI detection tools/checkers to determine if you have used AI.

PERMITTED

→ AI tools like Grammarly can be used to support writing by checking spelling, grammar, and punctuation errors.

→ AI tools can be used for support with coding.

→ AI tools can be used for support with brainstorming.

SUGGESTIONS

TEXT: For learning's sake, I suggest that you try not to use AI tools for word-for-word text generation that is directly turned in for writing assignments without translating into your own words. If you want to reference word-for-word text generated by AI, I suggest that you please put it in quotes and cite the AI generation software.

IMAGES: For speculative artwork ideas, you can use AI for brainstorming. I will specify when it can be used for final images.

*NOTE: Technology is changing rapidly today. If I need to change this policy, I will let you know right away.

no detection tools · trust-based · the question is always: is it supporting your voice or replacing it?

☘ Understanding How Work Is Assessed ▼

Your work is graded on comprehension, effort, engagement, and creative quality. Not on whether your opinion matches mine, not on whether the work is "right," and not on polish alone. A rough piece that argues something genuinely is worth more than a polished piece that argues nothing.

A · 100–90% · Excellent
Exemplary comprehension and application of the material. All work is completed, engaging, and exceptional in quality. The creative work demonstrates that you have genuinely wrestled with the ideas rather than illustrated them.

B · 89–80% · Good
Completed assignments with a clear grasp of key ideas and creative concepts. The project shows creative effort and engagement. The difference between a B and an A is usually depth — the reading is used rather than merely cited, and the argument is specific rather than general.

C · 79–70% · Satisfactory
Most assignments completed, but not all. The work lacks creative and aesthetic effort, or is underdeveloped or incomplete. A C usually means the student did the work but did not push past the first idea.

D · 69–60% · Unsatisfactory
Work is not completed as assigned. Substantial problems exist. Major components are missing. Writing shows lack of editing and poor grammar.

F · below 60% · Fail
Work is not submitted, or includes very few of the requirements.
